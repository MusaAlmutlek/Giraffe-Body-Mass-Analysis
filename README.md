# Giraffe-Body-Mass-Analysis
Title: Ecological Determinants of Body Mass in the Giraffe (Giraffa camelopardalis): A Linear Mixed-Effects Modelling Approach  

Abstract / Summary: This study investigates the environmental and biological drivers of body mass in the Giraffe (Giraffa camelopardalis). Using a Linear Mixed-Effects Model (LMM) on a dataset collected across multiple protected areas, we tested three key hypotheses regarding resource availability, water constraints, and predation pressure.

Key Findings:

Resource Availability: Rainfall and dense vegetation were positively correlated with body mass, supporting the hypothesis that bottom-up resource availability drives somatic growth.

Water Constraints: Distance to water showed a significant negative relationship with body mass, highlighting the energetic costs of travel for water-dependent herbivores.

Predation Pressure: The impact of predator density and lion presence varied, suggesting a complex interplay between selection for larger size and the non-consumptive effects of fear.

This analysis provides critical insights for conservation management, emphasizing the importance of maintaining water access and habitat quality to ensure the resilience of giraffe populations.
